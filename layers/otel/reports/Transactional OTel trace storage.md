# Make transactional trace search earn adoption

**FoundationDB Record Layer is a credible foundation for a fresh, selective OTel serving tier—not yet evidence of a faster or cheaper general observability database.** The recommended design keeps stable span identities and transactionally maintains purpose-built, typed custom-attribute postings, session membership and graph relationships. It indexes every admitted ordinary scalar rather than making customers predict a fixed allowlist, while keeping sensitive content behind separate policy and authorization. The decisive risks are replicated index bytes, resource/scope expansion, partition fanout and correct query/lifecycle semantics. Modern ClickHouse must be compared with its supported Map indexing, materialized fields, projections and compression, not an untuned scan. The canonical implementation design is [RFC-283](../rfcs/283-transactional-otel-trace-storage.md). At the original research stage, no prototype or benchmark had been run. A bounded immutable [executable kernel](../cmd/otelbench) has since been exercised; see its [scope and exclusions](../README.md#implemented-semantic-class) and retained [10k diagnostic receipts](../bench/evidence/2026-10-27/diagnostic-10k/) and [pipelined 100k receipts](../bench/evidence/2026-10-27/pipeline-100k/README.md). The repository remains pre-1.0.

## Exact custom attributes justify a purpose-built serving tier

The strongest hypothesis concerns tenant-scoped trace lookup, rare customer request/run identifiers, immediate search after durable acknowledgment and bounded session/graph navigation. Record Layer already supplies transaction-aware custom maintainers, owned index subspaces and online-build machinery. Its concrete validation, planner-candidacy, typed-scan and external lifecycle gaps are opportunities to improve the owned layer, not reasons to replace it. RFC-283 proposes a stable trace-first primary record plus per-span equality and numeric postings, with no global ID allocator, shared posting block or hot per-session counter. ([Repository investigation](../research_notes/Transactional%20OTel%20trace%20storage/repository.md))

Correctness determines the executor. Same-span conjunction intersects span identities; separate trace existentials first union witnesses across **all relevant buckets, shards and epochs**, then join trace identities. Parent ancestry, directed links and shared sessions remain different relations. Newest-first top-K requires a complete timestamp-ordered driver and a stopping frontier; trace-ordered postings cannot simply stop at K hits. One bounded result page can use an FDB snapshot, but later pages cannot retain that snapshot indefinitely. RFC-283 offers page snapshots and live continuation, not a fictitious durable read version. Cross-batch joins yield candidates until all witnesses, edges and authorization are revalidated in one bounded final transaction. ([FDB 7.3.77 limits](https://github.com/apple/foundationdb/blob/7.3.77/documentation/sphinx/source/known-limitations.rst), [FDB design notes](../research_notes/Transactional%20OTel%20trace%20storage/fdb-design.md))

Corrections and replays consult mandatory identity lifecycle state; retired/deleted identities cannot silently become live again. Persistent operation receipts return the original result after an unknown commit, even after a subsequent correction. Source journal records survive search-index rebuild/drop. Source-exclusive content uses generation-fenced publication, GC claims and reader pins; sensitive resource/scope overlays cannot linger in shared descriptors after deletion. These remain proposed protocols requiring failure-oracle tests, not verified behavior.

## Agent observability requires logs, provenance and content policy

The current conventions moved to `semantic-conventions-genai`. The design pins **Development** commit `e19717d65f890955777b044bca2a3cd71ae8b498`, not a stable specification or a claim about installed SDK behavior. Admitted typed/scoped data and producer/schema provenance survive alongside versioned projections. Opaque unknown fields require explicit protected capture authorization or rejection if they cannot be sanitized; preservation must not bypass content capture-off. Requested and actual model IDs differ; conversation identity is not trace identity; tool-call identity is not parentage. Missing token counts are not zero, and cache/reasoning/modality subsets must not be added again to inclusive totals. Vendor/custom cost fields require provenance and currency rather than a falsely standardized name. ([Pinned GenAI overview](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/e19717d65f890955777b044bca2a3cd71ae8b498/docs/gen-ai/README.md), [OTel/GenAI notes](../research_notes/Transactional%20OTel%20trace%20storage/otel-genai.md))

A span-only backend cannot cover `EVENT_ONLY` inference content and evaluation events. The product design therefore includes correlated OTLP logs, with explicit limits on deduplication when producers supply no stable event identity. Prompt/tool payload capture and indexing are independent opt-ins; metadata, content objects, existence queries, exports and deletion workflows all enforce authorization. Atomic span ingestion establishes a coherent observed revision, **not a complete distributed trace or conversation**. ([Pinned GenAI events](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/e19717d65f890955777b044bca2a3cd71ae8b498/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-events.md))

## Replicated bytes can defeat a latency advantage

The RFC models two populations rather than claiming an ideal compression ratio. Under explicit unmeasured encoding assumptions, ordinary service spans use **6,584 logical bytes/span**, while resource-heavy agent spans use **20,588**. At 100M spans, the **three-copy uncompressed logical-byte estimates under those assumptions** are **1.9752 TB and 6.1764 TB**, not physical lower bounds: compression can reduce them. Logs, backups, engine overhead, lifecycle tombstones, receipts and content add separate costs. The mutation ledger now includes amortized descriptor creation and separately charges content staging and correction value refreshes. Resource/scope payload interning does not eliminate their per-span postings. Normalized descriptor joins and immutable compressed history are therefore serious alternatives, but need correctness and cost evidence before promotion. ([RFC amplification ledger](../rfcs/283-transactional-otel-trace-storage.md#81-two-explicit-modeled-populations))

ClickHouse already offers sparse pruning, Map skip indexes, typed hot columns, projections and production text-index paths; these can materially change selective-query performance. Broad analytics and longer retention can favor CH or object storage even if FDB wins fresh operational lookups. The RFC specifies a seeded rare-key query and a proposed numeric deployment/load/price envelope, including queue stability, error and freshness gates. Approval and a frozen executable manifest are prerequisites, not assumed requirements. Both mixed-workload CH and an optimized exact-attribute alternative must handle previously unpromoted keys. Measurements include concurrent ingestion/query, skew, maintenance, p50/p95/p99 and visibility tails. An FDB win is disproved if tuned CH matches its p99 at equal sustainable load/budget or if accounting for replicated bytes and writes removes the advantage. ([ClickHouse OTel schema](https://clickhouse.com/docs/use-cases/observability/schema-design), [competitor notes](../research_notes/Transactional%20OTel%20trace%20storage/competitors.md))

## Conclusion

At the original research stage, existing FDB container ownership and a usable CH comparator had not been established, and no deployment was touched. The subsequent kernel and single-node, closed-loop diagnostics do not establish production readiness or a competitive win: sustainable ingest capacity and query tails under concurrent load remain unmeasured. Actual workload/SLO/cost targets, producer revision guarantees, privacy/deletion horizons and resource-index economics remain owner decisions. If those gates favor a hybrid, that is a successful outcome of this design: use transactions where fresh exact retrieval earns its cost, and columnar/object storage where scan efficiency and retention economics dominate.
