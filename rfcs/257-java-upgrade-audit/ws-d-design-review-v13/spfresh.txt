## P1: v13's own evidence runs contradict B, the same finding all four v12 reviewers raised

- **How B was set.** Design 1393-1402 and README:385-392 take the worst refit rate over `wsd-corner-1`, `v12-1` and `v12-2`, which is 1.02e-7 s per n·d. The v13 evidence runs that README item 30 binds to this design are worse:
  - `rfc257-v13-1.log:2126`: 437.6 ms at n=2000, d=1250, which is **1.75e-7**.
  - Inside the admitted corner, `:2134`: 265.0 ms at n=2000, d=980, which is 1.35e-7. A peel at the bound then takes about 2.65 s, not 2.0 s.
  - `:2120`: refit total 918.0 ms. Design 1404 says "at most 0.91 s", and 1405 and README:392 give the worst single refit as 170.6 ms.
- **By the design's own rule, B is about 1.14e7.** That admits n=2000 only up to d≤571 and n=1001 up to d≤1268. Both acceptance fixtures (W = 1.54e7 and 1.85e7) and the re-measured corner fall outside it.
- **Why it recurs:**
  - The calibration population leaves out the runs taken for this design.
  - Item 30 excludes timing lines from its identity check.
  - `v13-1` is about 1.5× slower across the board, which points to unrecorded load.
- **Needed:**
  - Derive B over every run the design binds, with the load average recorded for each run.
  - Restate the admitted corner, the fixtures and README item 29.
  - Pin the W = B edge: n=2000, d=980 gives exactly 1.96e7.

## P2: SPFresh foreground liveness rests on an unmeasured window

The claim (design 1026-1028; RFC-094:127-128) is that a SEAL→SPLIT window "closes within the split's two transactions, far inside ten attempts". The source contradicts this in three ways:

1. **The chunked drain spans many transactions** (RFC-094:333-340; `spfresh_split.go:414+`).
   - The parent stays SEALED across the plan transaction, every chunk transaction and the final one.
   - The plan publishes no changelog; only the final transaction does (`:484`).
   - The write path's SEALED arm never follows the children (`spfresh_write.go:361-363`).
   - The synthetic 1020 returns without the cache reload that the stale-route loop performs (`:410-419` vs `:115-148`).
   - RFC-094:333-334 says the 1M foreground fill reached exactly this path.
2. **Deletes stretch the window.** SPLIT's real read of the posting is aborted on purpose by concurrent updates and deletes (094:351-353; §12, 094:552-553), and it retries without bound.
3. **Ten attempts can pass quickly.** ExponentialDelay draws each delay uniformly in [0, current) (`ExponentialDelay.java:64-71`), so ten attempts can elapse in far less than 3.3 s.

**Paper lens.** LIRE keeps foreground updates independent of background rebalancing (SPFresh §3, as RFC-094 §2 and §6 map it). The synthetic 1020 is where a write waits on a split, and D-0 turns that wait into a failure. The design declares this only for a stalled seal.

**The fixtures (1103-1107) cannot show it as written:**
- The stalled-seal insert returns 1020 only when every routed candidate is SEALED (a lone posting, or a wave of splits). Otherwise it goes to the next-nearest ACTIVE centroid and succeeds (`spfresh_write.go:361-363`). The fixture must name that shape.
- "Completes within the bound" must be sequenced through the attempt observer, not by wall-clock timing.

**Census (5), 1072-1082,** leaves out `TestSPFreshForegroundFillBenchmark` (`bench/spfresh_sift_benchmark_test.go:462`). It is env-gated, writes batches through Run, and fails on any writer error.

**Needed:**
- Label the window as a hypothesis.
- Add fixtures for a chunked-drain window and for a window stretched by deletes.
- Run the fill benchmark before and after D-0, with attempt histograms.
- Decide and declare whether a drain of two or more chunks may fail writers during cold start.
