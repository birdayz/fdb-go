1. Isolating the Two Orthogonal Changes Between Today (A) and D-NG

Today's Go transport (A) differs from D-NG in two orthogonal ways:

  1. The 10–20 µs coalescing delay: A and D wait max(10 µs, 20 µs - sinceLastWrite) (via Linux hrtimer), whereas A0 (-coalesce-delay=0,0) and D-NG flush as soon as a frame is available.
  2. The write-acknowledgment wait & writer-turn machinery: In A and A0, every caller either takes a direct-write / writer-turn lock or sends a writeReq with an acknowledgment channel and waits for the socket write to complete before waiting for the reply. In D and D-NG (D1), callers append to a byte slice under a short mutex, signal the writer only on the 0 → 1 transition, and immediately wait on the reply channel.
What the 2×2 Factorial (A, A0, D, D-NG) Proves:
  • Latency at 8 & 64 workers is governed by the 10–20 µs delay: Removing the delay from today's transport (A → A0) jumps get100/64 from 48.5k to 100.0k ops/s (p50 1,262 → 618 µs) because each get100 transaction performs sequential hops (GRV + read waves), paying the 10–20 µs timer wait on every low-queue hop.
  • Throughput at 256 workers and client CPU/futex efficiency across all cells are governed by removing write-ack (A0 → D-NG):
    • On get100/256, D-NG reaches 257.7k ops/s at 27.0 µs/op client CPU and 0.06 futex/op, beating A0 (225.7k ops/s, 32.2 µs/op, 0.24 futex/op) and A (212.1k ops/s, 33.0 µs/op, 0.31 futex/op).
    • On get100/8, D-NG uses 140.5 µs/op client CPU vs A0's 158.7 µs/op and A's 179.0 µs/op (-21.5% client CPU).
    • Why does A0 beat D-NG specifically on closed-loop get100/64 (100k vs 52k ops/s), while D-NG+yield / D-NG-adap20 jump to 77k–80k ops/s? Because in A0, an idle connection writes directly on the sender's goroutine (0 goroutine hop) when no write is in flight, whereas in D-NG at intermediate concurrency (64 workers) the writer goroutine wakes on frame 1, writes 1–4 frames immediately, and goes back to sleep just as the next sender arrives; adding a single runtime.Gosched() (D-NG+yield) or 20 µs adaptive linger (DNGa20) keeps the writer hot across the burst and jumps throughput to 77k–80k ops/s while cutting client CPU to 39.1 µs/op (lower than A0's 42.1 µs/op)!
